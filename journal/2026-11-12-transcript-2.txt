  What the sheet says, for your late-pill comparison
  - Less than 48 hours since the pill was due: take it as soon as possible; no
    backup contraception is needed. CDC calls it "late" under 24 hours and
    "missed" from 24 to under 48 hours, but the advice is the same.
  - 48 hours or more (two or more pills missed): use condoms or abstain until
    pills have been taken for 7 days in a row.

  So the README's "up to 48 hours late without backup" matches the source. In
  the model, your 48 h scenario means two consecutive missed pills.

  The sheet was published in August 2024. I haven't checked whether CDC has
  changed or taken down that guidance since, so it's worth a look at

  How SHBG works in the model
  - SHBG rises while active pills are taken and drifts back down in the placebo 
    week. Its curve goes exactly through Alesse's measured values: 57, 81 and 93
    nmol/L on days 1, 6 and 21. It gets halfway to its plateau in about 3 days.
  - More SHBG means slower clearance. Levonorgestrel's clearance scales as (57 ÷
    SHBG)^1.33. I fitted the 1.33 to the measured exposure (AUC) on days 6 and
    21, and the model matches it within 4%.
  - Check not used in the fit: the week-3 low before the next pill comes out at
    2.04 ng/mL; Alesse measured 1.9.
  - Known shortfall: the week-3 peak is 4.9 ng/mL against 6.0 ± 2.7. That's
    within the spread but low, probably because the drug's distribution volume
    also shrinks over the cycle, which isn't modeled.
  - One thing I found that the label doesn't stress: unbound levonorgestrel also
    rose, and its unbound clearance fell. So the estrogen effect isn't only
    binding, and in the model SHBG stands in for the whole effect. That's stated
    in the code, the README and the page.
