## Decisions

- **Source: Edelman et al. 2009** (already in `sources/`), the paper named in the README's "Body weight" extension. It reports by **BMI**, not weight, so the page asks for **weight + height** and works out BMI. Units: kg/cm or lb/ft-in.
- **What changes with BMI: clearance only.** Edelman: levonorgestrel half-life 25.6 h (normal BMI, mean 21.9) vs. 52.1 h (obese, mean 37.3), volume of distribution and SHBG not different. Same volume and twice the half-life gives clearance × 25.6/52.1 = 0.49. In the model this scales `k10`.
- **Between and beyond the two groups**: clearance 1 up to BMI 21.9, 0.49 from 37.3 up (Edelman's BMI ≥ 40 subgroup looked like the rest of the obese group), smooth in between (log of clearance linear in BMI). The label studies are taken to be normal-BMI women.
- **Minipill**: no BMI data exist for norethindrone. Same change applied, labelled on the page as an untested assumption.
- **Match clearance, not half-life**: scaling `k10` by 0.49 raises levonorgestrel's model half-life ×1.75 (34 → 60 h), not ×2.03. One rate can't fix both in two compartments; clearance sets total exposure.
- **Website stays precomputed**: Python exports a grid of 9 BMIs (21.9–37.3); the page blends the two nearest. Page grew from 0.2 to 1.5 MB.
- **Hosting: GitHub Pages** from `main` / root, with a root `index.html` that forwards to `app/pill_comparison.html` and `.nojekyll` so the journal files aren't processed.

## Findings

- **Higher BMI makes late pills dip less in the model**, for both pills, because slower clearance keeps levels higher:

  | BMI | Minipill, 3 h late | Combined, 3 h late | Minipill, 1 missed | Combined, 1 missed |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 21.9 | 23% | 4% | 88% | 28% |
  | 30 | 20% | 3% | 83% | 21% |
  | 37.3 | 16% | 2% | 76% | 16% |

- **This is not what Edelman worried about.** Their concern is the start of each pack: obese women took about twice as long to reach steady levels (10 vs. 5 days) and had a lower first-day peak (1.9 vs. 2.5 ng/mL, not significant). Slower clearance alone can't lower the peak, so that effect isn't in the model. Said plainly on the page and in the README.
- **Combined-pill usual low rises with BMI**: 1.91 ng/mL at 21.9 → 4.06 ng/mL at 37.3.
- Couldn't read Edelman's Figure 2 (box plots are images; no PDF renderer, and installing one was blocked). The text gave enough: half-lives, "no Vd difference", Cmax.
