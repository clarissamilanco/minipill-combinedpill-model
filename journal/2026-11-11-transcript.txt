● The repo has no code yet and the README doesn't name a language, so I'm
  assuming Python, which is the usual choice for a simulation plus a web app
  like this. In Python, use the built-in logging module:

  import logging

  logging.basicConfig(level=logging.INFO, format="%(asctime)s %(levelname)s 
  %(name)s: %(message)s")
  logger = logging.getLogger(__name__)

  logger.error("Dose time %s is outside the pack (0–%s h)", t, pack_hours)

  If you're inside an except block, use logger.exception instead. It logs at
  error level and includes the full traceback:

  try:
      sol = solve_ivp(rhs, t_span, y0)
  except ValueError:
      logger.exception("ODE solve failed for %s", pill_name)
      raise   # re-raise unless you can really recover

  Some tips:
  - Call basicConfig only once, at the entry point (your app or a script), not
    inside src/ modules. In those modules, just use logger = 
    logging.getLogger(__name__).
  - Use %s placeholders, not f-strings. The message is then only built if it
    actually gets logged.
  - To also save logs to a file, add filename="run.log" to basicConfig.
  - Use logger.warning(...) for problems the model can live with. For example, a
    mass-balance check that's off by a small tolerance.

  If you meant writing down an error in your journal/ build log, or you're using
  a language other than Python, tell me and I'll adjust.

● I'm done reading: I went through your parameter sheet, the CDC sheet, and the
  pharmacokinetics sections of the norethindrone, Alesse and Levette labels.

  Start with the minipill alone: a one-compartment model of a single 
  norethindrone dose, plus a test that checks it against the exact (Bateman) 
  solution. It's the simplest piece, two of your planned checks depend on it,
  and it leads straight into your main question. You have 9 days until the
  November 20 launch, so get this core working before touching the app or the
  SHBG effect.

  Order I'd build in

  1. src/pk.py: a one-compartment model with oral absorption that takes a list
     of dose times, solved with scipy.integrate.solve_ivp. The "dose times"
     input is what will later let users take pills late or skip them.
  2. tests/test_pk.py: check it against the exact single-dose solution and check
     mass balance. Doing this first means you can trust everything you build on
     top of it.
  3. Norethindrone single dose vs. the label. Your absorption rate is easy: kₐ ≈
     3 /h gives a time to peak of 1.2 h when t½ = 7.7 h. Then run the 24-hour
     check. I worked it through by hand and it fails the way you predicted: the
     model drops about 8-fold from peak to 24 h, while the label shows a 37-fold
     drop.
  4. Add the second compartment if step 3 fails as expected. This is your
     project's first real finding.
  5. Levonorgestrel without SHBG over 21 days, then add SHBG.
  6. The late-dose comparison (3 h late vs. 48 h late), then build the app
     around it.

  Things in your sources worth knowing before you start

  - Alesse Table I gives you real data for calibrating SHBG. Your parameter
    sheet says this effect isn't reported directly, but the table has SHBG and
    levonorgestrel clearance on days 1, 6 and 21:

  | Day | SHBG (nmol/L) | CL/F (mL/h/kg) |
  |-----|---------------|----------------|
  | 1   | 57            | 53.7           |
  | 6   | 81            | 40.8           |
  | 21  | 93            | 28.4           |
    SHBG rises about 1.6-fold while clearance falls about 1.9-fold. You can fit
    a clearance-vs-SHBG relationship to these three points instead of tuning by
    eye. That makes the model much easier to defend.
  - Your SHBG check uses a target from a different pill. The "3- to 4-fold rise"
    comes from Levette. For Alesse (0.10 mg), which is the pill you validate
    against, the table shows about a 2-fold rise (peak 2.75 → 6.0 ng/mL, AUC 35
    → 68). Use Alesse's numbers for the Alesse check.
  - Your CDC source doesn't cover combined pills. cdc_missedpill_guidance.pdf is
    only the progestin-only pill sheet. The "<48 h / ≥48 h" columns in it are
    for drospirenone minipills. You'll need the CDC's combined-pill sheet to
    support the 48-hour rule.
  - Your parameter sheet is missing norethindrone bioavailability. The label
    gives F ≈ 65%, and the model needs it.
  - The label's volume can't produce the label's peak in one compartment. I
    assumed about 60 kg body weight: 4 L/kg × 60 kg = 240 L, which predicts a
    peak below 1,000 pg/mL against the measured 4,817. This is more evidence for
    a second compartment: the 4 L/kg describes the whole body, not the blood
    compartment. Fit V to the peak for now and note that it was fitted.
  - Small things: Levette gives levonorgestrel's half-life as about 24 h, which
    differs from Seasonale's 30 h and Alesse's 36 h. Your parameter sheet also
    cites a "Heather label" for norethindrone volume and clearance; the
    norethindrone label in sources/ gives the same numbers, so you could cite
    that instead.
