Combined pill model · levonorgestrel 0.10 mg (Alesse) · no SHBG yet

Levonorgestrel levels in the blood

The combined pill's progestin peaks about 1.6 hours after each pill and then falls slowly, with a half-life of about 34 hours. This version leaves out SHBG, the binding protein the pill's estrogen raises over the month, so it should match day 1 and fall short later in the pack.

The short version

  • Each pill sends the hormone up within about an hour and a half. After that the body clears it slowly: half is gone after about a day and a half. The minipill's hormone loses half in about 8 hours.
  • Because it clears so slowly, some of each pill is still there when the next one comes, so levels build up over the pack. By week 3 the low point before a pill is about 2.6 times what's left a day after the very first pill.
  • In the real pill, levels build up even more than this model shows. The pill's estrogen makes the body produce more of a protein (SHBG) that holds onto the hormone and slows its removal. That part isn't in the model yet.
  • So a late pill matters less: 3 hours late, levels dip about 6% below their usual low (the minipill dips 23%). A full day late, about 38%.

A full pack, without SHBG

In plain wordsLevels climb day by day as pills stack up, then drain away in the placebo week. Real measurements climb higher than the model. That missing piece is the estrogen effect.

One active pill a day for 21 days, then 7 placebo days. The open points are what Alesse measured on days 1, 6 and 21. The model only builds up from the 34-hour half-life, so by day 21 it sits well below the measured levels. That gap is what SHBG has to explain.
