Norethindrone 0.35 mg vs. levonorgestrel 0.10 mg + ethinyl estradiol (Alesse)

Minipill vs. combined pill

Every model built so far, on the same charts: the minipill's norethindrone and the combined pill's levonorgestrel, each with its one- and two-compartment versions; how the combined pill's estrogen changes things through SHBG; a month of pills; and what a late pill does to each.

The short version

  • Both hormones peak an hour or two after the pill. Norethindrone is half gone in about 8 hours; levonorgestrel in about a day and a half.
  • The combined pill's estrogen also makes the body produce more SHBG, a protein that holds onto levonorgestrel and slows its removal. So its levels build up to about twice as high by week 3.
  • A pill 3 hours late: the minipill dips 23% below its usual low, the combined pill about 4%. A whole pill missed: 88% vs. 28%.
  • Each pill's CDC limit lands at a similar dip: the minipill at 3 hours late dips 23%; the combined pill with one pill missed (still under CDC's 48-hour limit) dips 28%. Past 48 hours (two pills missed) it dips 49%.

SHBG: why the combined pill builds up

In plain wordsThe combined pill has two hormones, and one helps the other stick around. Its estrogen tells the liver to make more of a carrier protein, SHBG. Levonorgestrel rides on SHBG in the blood, and while it's held there the liver clears it more slowly. The minipill has no estrogen, so this never happens.

Estrogen (ethinyl estradiol) → liver makes more SHBG → more levonorgestrel held in the blood → cleared more slowly → levels build up over the pack

What SHBG is

Sex hormone–binding globulin: a protein made by the liver that carries sex hormones in the blood. Levonorgestrel binds it strongly; about half of the levonorgestrel in blood is on SHBG.

What the estrogen does

In the Alesse study, SHBG rose from 57 to 81 to 93 nmol/L on days 1, 6 and 21, about 60% higher by week 3. It levels off within about two weeks and drifts back down in the placebo week.

What that does to levonorgestrel

Over the same days, levonorgestrel's clearance fell almost by half (53.7 → 28.4 mL/h/kg), so levels on day 21 were about twice day 1's. That stored-up hormone is what carries the combined pill through a missed pill.

How the model does it: while active pills are taken, SHBG rises toward a plateau (half-way in about 3 days) and falls back in the placebo week. Levonorgestrel's clearance is scaled by (57 ÷ SHBG)1.33. The SHBG curve is fitted to the three measured values, and the strength (1.33) to the measured day-6 and day-21 exposure. The model then gives a low point of 2.0 ng/mL before the next pill in week 3, against 1.9 measured, which wasn't used in the fit.

Caveat: the label shows that the unbound (free) levonorgestrel also rose and its unbound clearance fell, so the estrogen effect isn't only binding. In the model, SHBG stands for the whole effect. The model's week-3 peak is also low (4.9 vs. 6.0 ± 2.7 ng/mL), likely because the drug's volume of distribution also shrinks, which isn't modeled.

When a pill is late

In plain wordsMake the day-15 pill late for both and compare. Each line is shown as a share of that pill's own normal daily low (100%), so the two can be read on one scale.

Pills on time until day 15; that pill is taken late (later pills stay on their usual time), skipped, or two pills are missed (the missed pill and the next one then taken together at +48 h, as CDC advises). CDC treats a minipill more than 3 h late as missed, but a combined pill needs no backup until 48 h.
